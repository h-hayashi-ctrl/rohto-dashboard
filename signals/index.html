How the agent works

Bracket Nowcaster never asks the language model for a probability. The model writes down one forecast: the number the market resolves on, with an error band, or how often an event happens. Fixed Python tools do the rest. They turn the forecast into a probability for each outcome, weigh it against the market price, and choose the trade size. The example values below come from the Hormuz trade.

  1. Tools1 · ScreenReads each market's prices, volume, deadline and rules. Skips closed markets and markets within 6 hours of the deadline.4 date brackets, 64 days left
  2. LLM2 · Forecast a numberThe only judgment step. A centre and error (sd) for the resolving figure, or a rate for "when will X happen". The sd can't go below a set minimum.0.1 qualifying transits per 30 days
  3. Tools3 · Price the bracketsReads the outcome labels as numeric or date ranges. Fat-tailed distribution for numbers, constant-rate arrivals for dates.6.1 / 9.2 / 4.1 / 80.5 %
  4. Tools4 · Weigh the marketBlends model and market price. The market gets more weight the more SIGS have traded and the closer the deadline. It gets less weight when the forecast rests on hard data.volume read as 0 → market weight 0
  5. Tools5 · Size the tradeQuarter-Kelly computed on the exact LMSR cost curve, so the agent's own price impact counts. It needs an edge of at least 3 pt, spends at most 10% of the budget, and checks the cost against the platform's quote.buy 0.2817 shares for 0.100 SIGS
  6. Tools6 · Pick across marketsRanks every candidate by expected log growth per SIGS. Then fills the usage limit and daily trade limit, with no theme taking more than 40% of the budget.1 trade per day allowed

How it differs from a simple agent

"Simple agent" here means the minimum the Signals spec asks for: a strategy prompt plus the platform's three fixed tools (fetch_target_markets, fetch_market_detail, calc_lmsr_cost). The language model then picks both the probability and the amount. This is a general comparison, not a description of any particular team's agent.

How much the market price counts, by trading volume

The agent's pool_with_market rule with liquidity b = 100 SIGS, about 50 days to the deadline, and model confidence 0.3 (a forecast that is mostly judgment). The market's weight rises with volume, up to a cap of 90%. Grey dots: where both trades actually sat. Because of the volume bug (below), the agent placed both at 0 SIGS and gave the market no weight.

What the decisions show

Bug

The agent reads every market as untraded

Both trade logs say "vol 0 SIGS, weight 0". SpaceX actually has — SIGS of volume, the most on the platform. With the real volume the market price would have carried —% weight, and the planned spend falls from — to — SIGS. The direction (buy NO) stays the same.

Cause: market_health looks for volume and deadline keys that the platform's fetch_market_detail evidently names differently.

Bug

Deadlines are missing too

The Hormuz log says market data "reported no deadline". That switches off the rule against opening positions within 6 hours of the close. The agent also gave the missing deadline as one reason for skipping the other markets.

Limits

The daily limit decides which trades happen

On 13 Nov the allocator chose several trades worth 0.495 SIGS, but only one could go out under the limit of — trade per day. — applied markets have no trades at all. The settings end on —.

Forecasts

Both forecasts are judgment, and the agent says so

Neither trade rests on a data series. Hormuz uses a guessed rate of 0.1 transits per 30 days. SpaceX uses a guessed year-end value of $1T–$1.5T, which the agent calls "low-confidence judgment". The strategy skipped markets where it had no grounded forecast, as intended.

The data checked afterwards backs both directions: Hormuz traffic is still about 4 ships a day, against roughly 65 before the crisis, and SpaceX is worth about $1.9T. That is above the agent’s guess but still 58% short of $3T. See the charts and reporting under each trade.

Trades and the reasoning behind them

For each trade: the market price of the outcome bought, the agent's own probability, and the blended belief it traded on. The written reasoning is a summary of the agent's log. Under it: the model against the market for every outcome, the data series the forecast turns on, and reporting that supports or cuts against the decision. The agent did not read these articles; they were collected afterwards to check its reasoning.

Sources and assumptions
  • Trades, reasoning, balance and limits were copied from the Signals Automatic Trading page and My Positions on 13 Nov 2026. These are fixed copies; they do not update.
  • Volumes, prices and deadlines come from the public Signals market list fetched the same day.
