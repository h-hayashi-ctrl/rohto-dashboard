How the agents work

The agents extract, calculate and flag problems. Final judgement on every assumption stays with me, and each value records who decided it (company disclosure, agent placeholder or student).

Inputs

Data gathered

  • Earnings releases (tanshin) and EDINET filings
  • yfinance prices, consensus, analyst targets
  • Financials of listed peers
  • Google Trends, JNTO, UV index, Rakuten
  • TDnet disclosures, ad & promotion spend of peers

Agent 1

Fundamentals

Checks the data, forecasts FY2027/3–FY2028/3, values Rohto on its own multiples and on peer multiples, then blends the two into a target price.

Agent 2

Alternative data

Turns this year's search, weather and tourism signals into a change in Japan revenue, EPS and target price, and suggests raising or lowering confidence. It does not set its own target.

Agent 3

Competitor intelligence

Compares Rohto with its peers on the same definitions, filed under the 3Cs and 4Ps, and proposes a premium or discount on Agent 1's peer multiple. It does not set its own target.

Debate

Moderator

The agents argue in rounds. A moderator weighs them and sets the final target price and confidence.

Output

Active view

Target, expected return, confidence-adjusted alpha and direction go to the seminar dashboard. A systemd timer reruns both agents every week.

The arguments, round by round

Round 1 · Agent 1

Own multiples put Rohto close to the current price. Peer multiples point higher but are pulled up by low-profit, high-P/E cosmetics names, and the ROE–P/B fit is weak, so peers get only half the weight. EPS is already below consensus, so no upgrade on unverified proxies.

Round 1 · Agent 2

Brand searches are strong, led by Rohto eye drops and Hada Labo, but Chinese visitors fell sharply and the inbound proxy cancels the gain. The net effect is a small cut to Japan growth, EPS and the target. The signals disagree, so confidence should come down.

Round 2 · Agent 1 rebuts

Agrees confidence should fall, but every elasticity and share is a placeholder. The suggested cut is smaller than the error in those assumptions, and the data windows don't line up. Keeps its target.

Round 2 · Agent 2 rebuts

Being below consensus is not evidence about this year's demand. A zero adjustment is a placeholder too, and accepting the brand upside while ignoring the China downside is asymmetric. Keeps the small cut and asks for the inbound share to be checked in company materials.

Round 3 · Final positions

Agent 1 holds its target, conceding the asymmetry point but not the cut. Agent 2 holds its small cut. Both agree the inbound share is the biggest unknown. After this debate I moved inbound to reference-only until it can be verified.
